Pricing-model shift

  • Seats to credits: ZoomInfo is moving per-seat customers to pre-bought consumption (Q2 call 2026-08-12) and says seat mix peaked in 2022 (Q1 call 2026-05-11). Cognism and Apollo meter in credits. ◇ our inference
  • Credits to actions and outcomes: Clay (Actions + Data Credits, unlimited seats), Rox (outcome-based and usage-based, Agent Actions, unlimited seats on Teams), Monaco (1M credits at $0.01, no seats, no platform fee). ◇ our inference
  • Outcome units at the CRM layer: HubSpot charges $1 per recommended lead (100 credits at $0.01). Salesforce charges 400 Flex Credits per Lead Worked ($2 at $500/100k), 20 credits per action ($0.10), or per conversation (salesforce.com · 2026-10-18; rate card 2026-08-31). ✔ verified
  • Hybrid seats + AI credits: Outreach, Common Room ($2,500/mo base with RoomieAI and Prospector credits), Unify ($20–60/seat with credits). Unlimited users plus credits with an ROI guarantee: UserGems ($40k/$75k/$150k per yr). Seat + platform fee with agents bundled: Gong. ◇ our inference
  • Agents are increasingly bundled at no extra cost to defend the base: ZoomInfo Agent Teams has no new SKU, and Apollo agents draw on existing credits. ◇ our inference
  • Sales Navigator is the outlier: pure seats ($119.99 / $159.99 / custom). ◇ our inference

LinkedIn data access and scraping

  • LinkedIn bans scraping and automation in its User Agreement. Proxycurl was sued Jan 2025 and later settled (law.com · 2026-10-18). ProAPIs got a permanent consent judgment on 2026-09-16, with data deletion (storage.courtlistener.com · 2026-10-18; news.bloomberglaw.com · 2026-10-18). ◐ third-party
  • Competitors make a point of being without scraping (ZoomInfo GTM.AI release, 2026-06-01). Fresh LinkedIn-native signals (job changes, posts, relationships) are scarce and legally defended. That is SN's moat, and it is also why agents built on others' data look for substitutes (Clay's 200+ providers). ◇ our inference
  • Clay syncs ad audiences to LinkedIn (pricing nav), so LinkedIn is a channel for them, not a data source. ◇ our inference

Where Sales Navigator is exposed

  1. No agent execution layer. Rivals sell does the work (Apollo), while SN suggests. ◇ our inference
  2. No MCP/API for AI assistants (unverified). Buyers who work inside Claude or ChatGPT reach Apollo, ZoomInfo, and Cognism data there, not SN. ? unverified
  3. Seat pricing against seatless and outcome models (Clay, Rox, Monaco). This hits hardest in startups and agentic teams that cut SDR headcount (a fraction of the headcount, Monaco pricing). ◇ our inference
  4. Bundled cost: Apollo starts at $49/seat/mo on annual billing with data, sequencing, and AI included. ◇ our inference
  5. CRM agents (Salesforce Hunter, HubSpot Prospecting Agent) source net-new contacts from ZoomInfo, Apollo, and other third parties, not LinkedIn, and SN data inside HubSpot is view-only, with no import. ◇ our inference
  6. Agent-native startups target SN's account-research use case directly (Actively, Rox). ◇ our inference

Where Sales Navigator is strong

  1. First-party, member-maintained profile and job-change data. Its legal enforcement record raises the cost of scraping alternatives. ◇ our inference
  2. Relationship graph: warm intros, TeamLink, Relationship Map (Sept 2026 release). ◇ our inference
  3. InMail and native engagement on the network where buyers actually are. ◇ our inference
  4. Distribution: LinkedIn and Microsoft (Teams alerts, Dynamics). Competitors are integrating into Microsoft Copilot, which SN's parent controls. ◇ our inference
  5. ZoomInfo weakness (NRR 89%, 20% layoffs, downmarket ACV down 12%) opens space in mid-market. ◇ our inference
